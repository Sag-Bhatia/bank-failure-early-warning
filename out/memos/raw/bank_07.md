# Bank 07: supervisory memo

**Dominant risk: rapidly deteriorating credit at a small bank** (8.1st size percentile). NPLs+OREO rose from 4.8% to 10.5% to 12.2% (97.5th percentile); the Texas ratio from 28.7% to 65.4% to 78.5% (99.6th). Past-due loans climbed from 0.21% to 0.88% to 1.13%. Reserve coverage is only 14% (0.5th), and net charge-offs remain high at 1.5%. CRE is just 1% of capital, so the problems sit elsewhere in the loan book.

**Liquidity, newly weaker.** The liquid-asset ratio fell from 35% to 29% to 14% and the run gap rose from 0.93 to 1.30 to 2.08 (82nd). Loans grew 24% this quarter. Uninsured deposits are 31%.

**Capital.** Leverage is 8.05% (7.2nd percentile). Mark-to-market capital moved from +1.1% to -0.6% to -1.3%; unrealized securities losses equal 56% of Tier 1. ROA is still 0.86%.

**Model view.** The failure model (0.99, 0.999, 1.0) and credit lens (about 0.99) agree: this is a classic credit case. The run lens is also high (0.818, 0.876, 0.867) as liquidity drains, adding a second front rather than disagreeing.

**Questions**
1. Which loan segments make up the 12.2% problem assets, and why do reserves cover only 14% of them?
2. Why did the bank grow loans 24% and run down liquidity while problem assets rose from 4.8% to 12.2%?
3. Is ROA of 0.86% credible at these problem levels; is interest on non-performing loans still being accrued?
