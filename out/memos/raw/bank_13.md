# Bank 13: supervisory memo

**Dominant risks: rate exposure in the loan book and uninsured funding at a very large, fast-growing bank** (99.7th size percentile). Estimated loan losses at market prices equal 130% of Tier 1 (96.5th percentile), up from 94% and 104%; long-dated assets are 7.3x Tier 1 (97.3rd). Mark-to-market capital fell from -1.36% to -3.13% to -4.75% (2.5th). Uninsured deposits are 68% (97.3rd), the run gap 4.45 (96.8th), the liquid-asset ratio 12.6%, and loans/deposits 95%.

**Growth.** Loans grew 23%, 24% and 24% (T-2 to T) while deposit growth slowed from 23% to 19% to 13%. Brokered deposits rose from 0.4% to 2.9% to 3.0%.

**Credit** is very clean: NPLs+OREO 0.07%, net charge-offs 0.0%, Texas ratio 0.59%, reserves 7.2x problem loans. CRE is 211% of capital.

**Capital.** Leverage 8.51% (13th) and Tier 1 risk-based 11.56% (11.5th) are below most peers.

**Model disagreement.** The run lens is high (0.894, 0.895, 0.905); the failure model is moderate (0.727, 0.667, 0.705), as expected for a model trained on credit failures facing a bank with almost no problem loans. The vulnerability is rate and funding: a deposit outflow could force recognition of market-value losses.

**Questions**
1. What is the rate profile of the long-dated loans, and how is it hedged given swaps are zero?
2. How will 24% loan growth be funded as deposit growth slows?
3. How concentrated are the uninsured depositors, and how much contingent funding is pre-arranged?
