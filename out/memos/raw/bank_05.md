# Bank 05: supervisory memo

**Dominant risks: thin capital, weak credit and long-dated assets at a small bank** (9.9th size percentile). Failure model 0.998, 0.996, 1.0 (T-2 to T); credit lens 0.997, 0.996, 0.995.

**Capital.** Book equity/assets was 1.17%, -0.82% and 0.65%, near the bottom of peers (0.7th percentile at T), against a reported leverage ratio of 8.96%. Mark-to-market capital is -5.38% (1.5th). Unrealized securities losses equal 93% of Tier 1 (98.4th) and long-dated assets 6.5x Tier 1 (94.4th).

**Credit.** NPLs+OREO 3.7% (95.2nd), Texas ratio 19.3% (97.9th), reserve coverage only 57% (5.5th). Net charge-offs are slightly negative (net recoveries) in every quarter despite elevated problem assets.

**Earnings.** ROA 0.15% and efficiency 94% (94.9th) leave little capacity to absorb losses.

**Funding.** Uninsured deposits are low (15%) and the run gap 0.49. Borrowings are 11.4% of assets (92.2nd). Swaps are not reported.

**Model disagreement.** The failure model is at its maximum; the run lens is mid-range (0.654, 0.657, 0.603) because deposits are mostly insured. The risk is solvency, not a deposit run; reliance on borrowings is the funding item to watch.

**Questions**
1. How does the bank reconcile near-zero or negative book equity with an 8.96% leverage ratio, and could it be forced to realize its securities losses?
2. Why are charge-offs negative while problem assets are 3.7% and reserves cover only 57%?
3. What collateral backs the borrowings, and how would a valuation haircut affect liquidity?
